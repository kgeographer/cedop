## On the variable list in Task 1

The list is comprehensive but worth one more pass against the actual L8 schema to catch a couple of omissions. `relief_range_m` and `relief_position` are derived Band A variables present in the current payload and worth characterizing — `relief_position` in particular (the point's elevation within the basin's min-max range) has interesting distributional properties that might be worth looking at. `lith_class` and `biome` and `ecoregion` are categorical and won't take histograms, but their frequency distributions globally (how skewed is the biome distribution? are there rare lithology classes that only appear in a handful of basins?) are part of the characterization, and the document currently defines the task over scalars only. A short amendment — "for categorical variables, compute frequency distributions and entropy" — would round it out. Same for `wetland_class`, `pnv_majority`, `freshwater_ecoregion_name`.

`pnv_shares` is structurally interesting because it's neither scalar nor simple categorical — it's a dictionary of shares summing to 100. Characterizing its diversity (how many basins have a single dominant PNV at >95%? how many are mixtures?) is a separate kind of question and probably warrants its own small task, or at least a note.

## On the L6/L8 split in Task 2

The document says to cross-tabulate by basin level to identify scale-degradation patterns. Good, but worth being explicit that L6 has roughly a twentieth the basin count of L8 (~10,000 vs ~190,000), which means L6 distributions will look different even for variables that don't "degrade" per se — they'll just have fewer samples and coarser aggregation. Separating "statistical artifact of smaller N" from "genuine scale-degradation of the variable" will matter, and it's a thing that's easy to conflate if the comparison isn't done carefully. A sentence acknowledging this would help whoever does the work (possibly future-you in July) remember to look for it.

## On Task 5's comparison clause

"Comparison with the existing 20-cluster result from the workbench (which used bands A–D)" is worth flagging as an interesting bit of methodological reflexivity rather than a casual cross-check. The workbench 20-cluster result is an earlier version of your own work using a different variable set; comparing the new clustering against it is half validation, half archaeology of your own methodological choices. Worth being conscious that you're doing both, because some of the "why did the cluster assignments change" questions will resolve as "I changed what I was clustering on" rather than as substantive findings about the data.

A concrete operational note on this task: k-means and HDBSCAN give quite different cluster structures (k-means imposes spherical equal-variance clusters; HDBSCAN finds density-based clusters of varying size and can leave noise points unassigned). Running both and comparing is genuinely informative for deciding which is right for your use case; a 20-cluster HDBSCAN is not the same object as a 20-cluster k-means even on identical data. If the goal is a stable typology, you'll probably want to converge on one method with justification rather than carrying both forward. Worth settling during Task 5, not later.

## One addition worth considering

A sixth task that's implicit in what you're doing but not named: **geographic coverage and sampling-bias characterization**. BasinATLAS covers terrestrial Earth uniformly, but the *basins that matter for historical scholarship* are heavily clustered in specific regions (Mediterranean, East Asia, South Asia, Mesoamerica, Nile valley, etc.). If the D-PLACE and Cliopatria and Reba places you'll eventually be testing against are concentrated in certain global regions, knowing the signature-space distribution of those regions versus the global distribution tells you where your correspondence experiments have statistical power and where they don't. A map showing "where in environmental signature space do D-PLACE societies actually live, and how does that compare to the global basin distribution" is a useful artifact for the methods paper and it's cheap to produce once Task 1 is done.
