# Introduction

How should we describe the environmental context of a place in a way that is both computationally tractable and analytically meaningful for historical and cultural research? While geographic information systems have made it routine to associate locations with environmental attributes—climate, elevation, land cover—such approaches typically treat environment as a static overlay: a set of values sampled at or near a point. This model is sufficient for many applications, but it is poorly suited to questions in which environment is understood as *relational, processual, and scale-dependent*, as in the long-standing geographical tradition of cultural landscape.

This paper introduces EDOP (Environmental Dimensions of Place), a computational framework for constructing and comparing environmental “signatures” of places. EDOP is not a fixed classification or dataset, but a **parameterized, process-aware model** that generates environmental descriptions based on configurable assumptions about spatial units, variable selection, scale, and environmental processes. It is designed to support exploratory and comparative research on environment–culture relations, particularly in historical contexts where both environmental conditions and territorial extents vary over time.

The motivation for EDOP arises in part from the mismatch between the kinds of environmental questions scholars wish to ask and the representations typically available to them. Consider, for example, the territorial expansion of the Northern Song polity in 10th-century China. As the polity extended southward over several decades, it incorporated regions characterized by markedly different hydro-climatic regimes, including substantially wetter and more productive landscapes. Describing this shift in environmental context is not simply a matter of sampling a few variables at a set of points. It requires decisions about how to define the relevant spatial units, how to aggregate or compare environmental conditions across changing extents, and how to account for processes—such as hydrological flow—that connect distant locations. The resulting description is therefore not given in advance, but constructed.

EDOP addresses this problem by treating environmental characterization as a modeling exercise. At its core is the concept of an environmental signature: a vectorized representation of environmental conditions associated with a place or region, derived from multi-scale geospatial datasets. In the current implementation, these signatures are constructed using HydroATLAS sub-basins as primary spatial units, combined with variables describing climate, hydrology, terrain, land cover, and related factors. However, the choice of units, variables, and transformations is explicitly parameterized. Different configurations yield different signatures, reflecting alternative assumptions about what constitutes the “environment” of a place.

A key feature of the model is its distinction between *local* environmental conditions and those derived from spatial relationships, particularly upstream hydrological networks. By leveraging the directed graph structure of river basins, EDOP can incorporate environmental influences that originate beyond the immediate vicinity of a location. This enables a more process-aware characterization of place, in which environmental context includes not only what is present locally but also what flows into and shapes that location over space.

The framework also confronts several well-known challenges in geographic information science, including the modifiable areal unit problem (MAUP), scale sensitivity, and the need for defensible methods of spatial interpolation and aggregation. Rather than attempting to eliminate these issues, EDOP makes them explicit and exposes key modeling choices as parameters. In doing so, it allows users to explore how different assumptions affect the resulting environmental signatures and any subsequent analyses.

A working prototype of EDOP has been implemented as an API-driven service, integrating HydroATLAS, ecoregional classifications, and additional environmental datasets within a PostgreSQL/PostGIS and vector-similarity framework. This system supports the generation of environmental signatures for arbitrary coordinates, the comparison of places based on similarity in environmental space, and the aggregation of environmental characteristics over historical regions. It is intended as both a research tool and a foundation for further methodological development, including integration with cultural datasets such as D-PLACE and historical gazetteers.

In framing environmental context as a parameterized, process-aware construct, EDOP seeks to provide a more flexible and transparent basis for analyzing the relationships between environment and human activity. The goal is not to produce a single authoritative description of place, but to enable the construction, comparison, and evaluation of multiple plausible descriptions, each grounded in explicit modeling choices.
